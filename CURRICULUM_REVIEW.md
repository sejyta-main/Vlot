# Curriculum review and sign-off

Status: **editorial draft; independent professional review pending**

Vlot now contains a complete technical A1–B2 path: 28 units, 316 sentence targets, four independently scheduled skills, communicative missions, AI roleplay, and internal checkpoints. “Complete” here means that every CEFR stage and major communicative mode has an implemented path. It does not mean externally certified.

## Alignment basis

The curriculum uses the Council of Europe’s action-oriented model and covers reception, production, interaction, mediation, strategies, fluency, phonological control, grammatical accuracy and sociolinguistic appropriateness.

Primary reference: [CEFR Companion Volume, Council of Europe (2020)](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4).

The advanced progression deliberately moves from B1 handling of routine/non-routine matters, connected experience, summaries and opinions to B2 sustained argument, negotiation, critical evaluation, mediation and spontaneous repair.

## Required reviewer profile

At least one reviewer should:

- be a qualified NT2 teacher or Dutch-language curriculum specialist;
- have current familiarity with adult CEFR assessment;
- understand Belgian Standard Dutch and the boundary between broadly useful Flemish usage and local dialect;
- have no authorship conflict, or document that conflict explicitly.

A second reviewer from a different Belgian region is strongly preferred for audio/register decisions.

## Unit-level checklist

For every unit, record pass, revise, or reject for:

- claimed CEFR difficulty;
- communicative usefulness and task authenticity;
- grammatical accuracy;
- natural Belgian Standard Dutch;
- register (`u`, `je`, and colloquial `ge/gij`) and consistency;
- English meaning and accepted alternatives;
- plausible distractors;
- progression from model to controlled retrieval to free production;
- cultural accuracy and avoidance of stereotypes;
- accessibility for an adult learner living in Belgium.

## Assessment checklist

- Checkpoints sample reception, production and interaction rather than vocabulary recognition alone.
- Parallel forms are comparable enough to measure change.
- Cut scores are not marketed as official certification.
- Speech-transcript measures are clearly separated from acoustic pronunciation measures.
- Can-do milestones require repeated performance after a delay.
